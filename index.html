Ehsan Farahbakhsh

Research Fellow · Research School of Earth Sciences, The Australian National University

Jaeger Building, 142 Mills Road

Research School of Earth Sciences

ANU, Canberra ACT 2601, Australia

I use machine learning and data science to find where and when ore deposits form. My work links plate tectonic reconstructions and geodynamic models to the deposit record, so that prospectivity models can predict mineralisation through deep time, not just across today’s map.

At ANU, I work with Prof. Rhodri Davies in the Computational & Observational Geodynamics group. I am building the machine learning framework for GEM-Cu (Geodynamic Environments for Mineralisation of Copper). GEM-Cu is an industry project with Rio Tinto, The University of British Columbia and Imperial College London, which brings mantle convection models into copper exploration.

Before ANU, I spent five years as a Research Associate in the EarthByte Group at The University of Sydney with Prof. Dietmar Müller. There I developed 2D and spatio-temporal prospectivity frameworks for the PLATO and STELLAR industry projects, lectured undergraduate courses and supervised research students. I hold a PhD in Mining Engineering (Mineral Exploration) from Amirkabir University of Technology.

Research interests

  • Spatio-temporal mineral prospectivity modelling (porphyry copper and sediment-hosted systems)
  • 3D and 2D prospectivity modelling where labelled data are scarce
  • Geophysical and geochemical data analysis, including anomaly detection and crustal thickness estimation
  • Geological remote sensing with computer vision and deep learning

I am a recipient of the 2025 ASEG Early Achievement Award and the 2025 COSPAR Outstanding Paper Award for Young Scientists.
